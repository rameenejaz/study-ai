Principles of Business Management — Lecture 5

Management is the process of planning, organising, leading and controlling an organisation's resources to achieve its goals efficiently and effectively. Henri Fayol, a French mining engineer, described these management functions in 1916.

Planning is the function of setting objectives and deciding in advance how to achieve them. A strategic plan covers the whole organisation for three to five years, while an operational plan describes day-to-day activities. SWOT analysis is a planning tool that examines an organisation's internal strengths and weaknesses and its external opportunities and threats.

Organising is the function of arranging people and resources into a structure that carries out the plan. An organisational chart shows the formal reporting relationships in a company. The span of control is the number of employees who report directly to one manager. A tall structure has many levels of management and a narrow span of control, whereas a flat structure has few levels and a wide span of control.

Leading means motivating, directing and communicating with employees. Abraham Maslow proposed a hierarchy of needs in 1943, which ranks human needs from physiological needs at the bottom to self-actualisation at the top. Frederick Herzberg separated hygiene factors, such as salary and working conditions, from motivators, such as recognition and achievement.

Controlling is the function of measuring performance, comparing it with standards and taking corrective action. A key performance indicator (KPI) is a measurable value that shows how well an organisation is achieving an important objective.

Marketing is the activity of identifying customer needs and satisfying them profitably. The marketing mix, often called the 4Ps, consists of product, price, place and promotion. Market segmentation divides a broad market into smaller groups of customers with similar needs.

Revenue is the total income a business receives from selling goods or services. Profit is the amount left after all expenses have been subtracted from revenue. Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of a business over a period. A business can be profitable on paper and still fail if it runs out of cash. Return on investment (ROI) measures the gain from an investment relative to its cost.

A sole proprietorship is a business owned by one person who has unlimited liability for its debts. A partnership is owned by two or more people who share profits and responsibilities. A corporation is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders, and its owners have limited liability.
