Sports Science Elective — Association Football (Soccer) Basics

Association football is a team sport played between two teams of eleven players, including one goalkeeper on each side. The objective is to score more goals than the opponent by moving the ball into the opposing team's net. A standard professional match lasts 90 minutes, divided into two halves of 45 minutes, with added time for stoppages.

The Laws of the Game are maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB), while FIFA governs the sport worldwide. FIFA was founded in Paris in 1904. The first FIFA World Cup was held in Uruguay in 1930, and Uruguay won the tournament.

The offside rule prevents attackers from waiting near the opponent's goal. A player is in an offside position if any part of the head, body or feet is nearer to the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent. Being in an offside position is not an offence by itself; the player must also become involved in active play.

A penalty kick is awarded when a defending player commits a direct free-kick offence inside their own penalty area. The penalty mark is 11 metres from the goal line. A yellow card is a caution given by the referee for unsporting behaviour or persistent fouls, and a red card sends a player off for the rest of the match.

The video assistant referee (VAR) is a match official who reviews decisions using video footage. VAR can only intervene for goals, penalty decisions, direct red cards and mistaken identity.

Common team formations include 4-4-2 and 4-3-3. Pressing is a defensive tactic in which players quickly close down opponents to win the ball back high up the pitch. Possession football is a style that focuses on keeping the ball through short passes.

Physical conditioning matters because a professional outfield player can run more than 10 kilometres in a single match. Aerobic fitness supports repeated high-intensity sprints, and recovery between matches reduces the risk of injury.
